## **The Dash (Тире)**

In Russian, a dash (—) is used between the subject and a noun that describes or identifies it when the verb "to be" is supposed to be there.

This commonly happens when both the subject and the noun predicate are nouns.

**Examples:**

    Томас — учитель. = Thomas is a teacher.

    Мой друг — капитан. = My friend is a captain.

The dash represents the missing verb "is".

**Note:** When the subject is a pronoun, such as он (he) or она (she), the dash is normally not there.

**Examples:**

    Он учитель. = He is a teacher.

    Она доктор. = She is a doctor.



## **Pronouns and Questions**
In Russian, they don't really use the verb "to be" (am/is/are) is usually not there in present tense. When you form a question the word order often stays the same, it's indicated by the question mark. This works on these words that I've learned:
| Russian | Statement | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Это | This is | Is this? |
| Я | I am | Am I? |
| Он | He is | Is he? |
| Она | She is | Is she? |
| Ты | You are | Are you? |


### **The Letter Г**
The Russian letter "**Г**" normaly sounds like a Dutch "g". However, when it appears at the end of a word it's often pronounced as a "**k**"

**Example:**
**Друг** = Friend - You pronounce this as "Droek" instead of "Droeg".

This is called "final consonant devoicing", it happens in a lot of languages also in Dutch, an example for a Dutch word is "hond", where you pronounce the d as a t.


### **The Letter О**
In Russian, the pronunciation of О depends on whether it is stressed or unstressed.
When О is unstressed, it is usually reduced to an "a" sound.

**Examples:**
**Спасибо** = Thank you             (**Pronunciation:** spa-SEE-ba)
  - The stress is on СИ.
  - The final О is unstressed and sounds more like "a". 
**Это** = This / This is            (**Pronunciation:** e-TAH)
  - The first syllable is stressed. 
  - The final О is unstressed and sounds more like "a".

They call this vowel reduction, in Russian this is called аканье (akanye).


### **Connected Speech**
This is when two words are spoken together, the vowels can flow into eachother.

**Example:**
**Кто он?** = Who is he?
  - The final "O" in Кто and the first "O" in Он flow together when you pronounce it. Making them sound like a continuous vowel "ktoon" instead of "kto on".
